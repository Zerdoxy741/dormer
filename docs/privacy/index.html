Privacy

Dormer keeps almost everything on your Mac. Here is exactly what leaves it, and when.

Updated November 26, 2026

The short version

Questions about your data: info@dormernotch.com.

Dormer has no account and no ads. It doesn't track you: the only thing it sends on its own is anonymous counts ("one more install", "one more update", "one more Mac used Dormer today"), with no identifier, and you can turn them off. Almost everything stays on your Mac. When a feature needs the internet, it sends only what it needs, and only if you turned it on.

Permissions

macOS asks for each permission when a feature needs it. You can say no: only the features that need it won't work.

  • Accessibility: to show volume and brightness in the notch instead of macOS's own overlays, and to follow calls and ChatGPT. Dormer doesn't read what you type.
  • Mac's sound: to move the equalizer with the music. The sound is analyzed live; nothing is recorded.
  • Music and Spotify: to show and control the current song.
  • Calendar and Reminders: for your next events and to snooze a reminder from the notch. Nothing leaves your Mac.
  • Microphone (optional): only for Shazam, for a few seconds, when you ask.
  • Location (optional): for the weather, rounded to about 1 km.
  • Bluetooth and local network: for the AirPods animation and to find nearby Macs running Dormer (Listen together).
  • Screen recording (optional): only if you turn on "see the screen" when asking Claude or ChatGPT, or "It looks at your screen" for the companion. In that second case, Apple's on-device AI describes the image on your Mac, then forgets it: nothing is sent. Both options are off by default.

Clipboard

Clipboard history stays in memory: it's never written to disk, never sent, and it's gone when you quit Dormer. It skips passwords: copies that password managers mark as concealed, and anything copied from Keychain Access, the Passwords app, 1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC, Dashlane, LastPass, Proton Pass and a few others. You can turn it off in settings.

Diagnostics

When you click Settings › General › Report a problem… and then Send, Dormer sends a diagnostic to its creator. Nothing leaves without that click, and you can read everything before it's sent.

What's sent:

  • what you write, and your first name if you give it (optional);
  • Dormer and macOS versions, Mac model, language;
  • permissions granted to Dormer (Accessibility, Calendar);
  • Dormer's settings: numbers, switches and short keywords only;
  • Dormer's recent logs and a summary of its last crash, if any.

Removed before sending: file names, paths, your user name and full name, email addresses, links, Bluetooth and IP addresses, first names in device names ("Anna's AirPods"), the content of your images and of on-screen text, clipboard content, and free text in settings (city, song, companion name…). The code that does this cleaning is public.

The signature: each diagnostic carries a signature like D-7F3A-91C2. It's drawn at random on your Mac and only groups your diagnostics together. It has no link to your Mac or to you.

Where it goes: the diagnostic passes through diag.dormernotch.com, a small service hosted by Cloudflare that keeps nothing and doesn't log the content. It's then stored in a private GitHub repository (GitHub, Inc., United States) that only Dormer's creator can read. Your IP address is only used to limit sends per minute against spam. It isn't stored with the diagnostic.

How long: each diagnostic is deleted automatically one year after it was sent, or sooner if you ask.

Why: to understand and fix the problem you report. That's its only use, with the consent you give by clicking Send.

Anonymous statistics

To know how many people use Dormer, the app sends to diag.dormernotch.com:

  • on the very first launch: "installed", with the Dormer and macOS versions;
  • on the first launch after an update: "updated", with the new version;
  • at most once per day you use Dormer: "active today", with the same versions;
  • if you answer the "Is everything working?" question, asked once after your first day: 👍 or 👎, with the Dormer version.

Nothing else: no identifier, no serial number, no name, no settings. The service only adds 1 to a counter for the day (for example "November 26: 12 active Macs"). Your IP address is only used to limit sends against spam and isn't stored. So there is no way to know who sent what, or to link two days together. The code is public.

To turn it off: Settings › General › Send anonymous statistics.

The dormernotch.com website

The website uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits: page views, country, browser, referring site. There are no cookies and no tracking across pages or sites, and no data is used to recognize you or for ads. Only totals are visible.

Features that use the internet

  • Updates: Dormer asks GitHub whether a new version exists, and only installs an update signed with the same certificate as the installed app.
  • Pro license: your license key is checked with Polar, which handles purchases.
  • Weather: the Mac's approximate location (to about 1 km) or the city name is sent to Open-Meteo.
  • Lyrics and artwork: the song title and artist are sent to lrclib.net and Apple's iTunes search.
  • Shazam: a sound fingerprint, not the recording, is sent to Shazam (Apple) to recognize the song.
  • Asking Claude or ChatGPT: the question goes through the Claude Code or ChatGPT tool installed on your Mac, with your own account. Dormer has no server in between. A screenshot is attached only if you turned on "see the screen".
  • Listen together: Macs talk directly, encrypted, over the local network or Bluetooth. No server is used.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete your diagnostics at any time. Email info@dormernotch.com with your signature (shown in the diagnostic window). To get a new signature, reset Dormer's settings. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

The controller of this data is Dormer's publisher: see the legal notice.
