What macOS gives you for volume

Before adding anything, it helps to know exactly what the system already does, because several of its controls are easy to miss.

Apple's Mac Help lists three built-in ways to change volume: the volume keys or Control Strip, the Sound slider in the menu bar or Control Center, and the volume controls inside certain apps, such as the Apple TV app.

A few details worth knowing:

  • Small steps. Each press of a volume key moves one step. Apple's keyboard shortcuts page documents Option–Shift–Volume Up or Down for smaller increments, which is handy when one step is too loud and the next too quiet.
  • Quick jump to settings. Holding Option while you press a volume key opens Sound settings.
  • Pin the slider. Apple's Control Center guide says the Sound control adjusts volume and switches which speaker or device your Mac uses for output. You can Control-click it and choose Add to Menu Bar to keep it visible.
  • Alert volume. In Sound settings, alert volume is relative to your Mac's output volume, so lowering the master level also quiets alert sounds.

In System Settings > Sound > Output you choose the device and then use Output volume, Mute or Balance. Input has its own volume and a level meter for your microphone.

Why there is no real mixer on a Mac

Windows has long shown a list of running apps with one slider each. On a Mac, Apple's own documentation describes a single output volume with app controls that cannot exceed it. Press coverage in 2026 noted that Apple still has not built a Mac volume mixer, which is why a category of third-party utilities exists.

The practical effect is easy to recognize:

  • You are listening to a playlist while a meeting app plays at full blast.
  • A notification sound from a chat app is much louder than the video you are watching.
  • You want a podcast louder than background music, but both share one slider.

The workaround most people use is lowering the master volume and then fiddling with volume controls inside each app, when the app has one. That works for a few apps. It breaks down for browser tabs, chat apps and anything without its own slider.

What a good Mac volume mixer should do

Whether you are a student juggling lecture recordings and study music, or a professional on calls all day, the same few things matter:

  3. No account and no fuss. A volume tool should not ask you to sign up.
  4. Fits the rest of your routine. Volume control is something you use for seconds at a time. It works best next to the other things you check constantly: what is playing, what is on the calendar, what you just copied.

How to get louder sound and a better balance

"How do I make the volume louder?" is a common follow-up. In macOS, app-level controls cannot exceed your Mac's output setting, so a quiet app is often limited by the master level, not by itself. Work through this order:

  1. Raise the master volume. Use the volume keys, or Option–Shift–Volume Up for finer steps.
  2. Check the output device. In System Settings > Sound > Output, confirm you are using the speaker or headphones you expect. Apple notes the Sound control in Control Center also lets you switch devices.
  3. Check Balance. The Output pane has a Balance slider. If one side is quieter, it may simply be shifted.
  4. Check the app's own volume. Some apps, such as Apple TV, have an in-app slider that sits on top of the system level.
  5. Boost only the app you need. With MixVol, lift a quiet app above 100% and trim the loud one until they match, instead of pushing the whole Mac louder.

If the quiet source is a video or call, level-matching between apps is usually all you need, and it keeps your ears safe from sudden jumps when you switch sources.

When an app is missing or will not change

A mixer lists apps that are producing sound at that moment. If something is missing:

  • Start playback. An app that is paused or silent will not appear until it plays audio, unless you turn on the option to show all apps.
  • Check the master level and mute. If Mute is on in Sound settings, or the output volume is very low, every app will sound quiet no matter where its slider sits.
  • Confirm the output device. Audio might be going to a different speaker or headphones than the one you are listening to, including an output you picked for that app in the mixer.
  • Look for an in-app control. Some players have their own volume slider that stacks on the system level.
  • Restart the app that is playing. Reopening it makes it register its audio again.
  • Update to the latest release. The Dormer changelog lists each version, and updates install from Dormer's settings.
